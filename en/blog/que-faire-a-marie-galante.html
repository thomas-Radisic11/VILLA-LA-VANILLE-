What to do in Marie-Galante? 9 must-sees for a perfect stay

Empty beaches, rum distilleries, windmills and cliffs: our pick of the best things to do in Marie-Galante, the island of a hundred windmills.

One hour by boat from Guadeloupe, Marie-Galante has kept its own pace. No big hotels, little traffic, sugar cane fields as far as the eye can see. People come for the sea, the rum and the gentle way of life. Here are our nine must-sees, all within a thirty-minute drive of the villa.

1. White sand beaches

Marie-Galante has some of the finest beaches in the archipelago. Anse Canot, near Saint-Louis, and La Feuillère beach, in Capesterre, offer fine sand and turquoise, often calm water. In the morning, you may have them almost to yourself. Bring a mask and snorkel: the sea floor is already beautiful a few metres from the shore.

2. Agricultural rum distilleries

Marie-Galante rum is famous across the Caribbean. Three distilleries welcome visitors: Bielle, Bellevue and Poisson, which makes Père Labat rum. During the cane harvest, you can watch the mills crush the stalks and smell freshly pressed cane juice. A tasting is a must, in moderation.

3. The windmills, symbols of the island

Marie-Galante is nicknamed "the island of a hundred windmills". The old stone windmills were used to crush sugar cane. Most now stand in ruins among the fields. The restored Bézard windmill has its sails and roof back: it is one of the most photographed sights on the island.

4. Habitation Murat

Close to Grand-Bourg, this former sugar estate is now an eco-museum. Its grand master's house, windmill and gardens tell the story of the island's sugar industry and of the women and men who made it. An ideal visit to understand Marie-Galante, just a few minutes from the villa.

5. Gueule Grand Gouffre

On the north coast, the Atlantic has carved a huge chasm into the cliff, linked to the sea by a rock arch. On days with swell, the waves crash into it. The site is spectacular, especially late in the day. Stay behind the railings: the cliff edges are slippery.

6. Ox carts

Marie-Galante is one of the few islands where you still see carts pulled by oxen, especially during the cane harvest. They are part of the island's identity and are even celebrated at festivals and competitions.

7. Under the sea

Snorkelling, diving, boat trips or fishing: there are many ways to discover the sea. The reefs are home to turtles, lobsters and tropical fish. Our concierge can book a boat trip for you with local partners.

8. Marie-Galante cuisine

Try bébélé, the island's traditional dish made with tripe, green banana and vegetables, grilled fish and lobster, coconut sorbet churned by hand, and coconut jam. The small beachfront restaurants are the best places to eat.

9. The Terre de Blues festival

Every spring, usually around the Pentecost weekend, the Terre de Blues festival fills the island with blues, jazz and Caribbean music. The atmosphere is unique, but accommodation goes fast: book several months ahead.

Planning your stay

Marie-Galante is reached by ferry from Pointe-à-Pitre, in about one hour, or by small plane. Once there, a rental car is essential to explore the island at your own pace. Allow at least a week to see everything without rushing: Marie-Galante is best enjoyed slowly.
