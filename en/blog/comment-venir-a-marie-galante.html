How to Get to Marie-Galante: Boat, Plane and Car Rental

Boat from Pointe-à-Pitre, short flight or car ferry: all our tips for getting to Marie-Galante easily and planning a smooth arrival on the island.

Marie-Galante takes a little effort to reach, and that is part of its charm. The island has no direct link to mainland France: you always travel through "mainland" Guadeloupe first, then cross the channel by boat or small plane. None of it is complicated. Here is everything you need to know to get to Marie-Galante with peace of mind, from landing in Guadeloupe to picking up your car keys.

Step one: getting to Guadeloupe

From mainland France, long-haul flights land at Guadeloupe Pôle Caraïbes airport in Pointe-à-Pitre (Les Abymes). Several airlines fly from Paris, and some from other French or European cities depending on the season. Allow around eight hours of flying time.

Our tip: if your flight lands in the late afternoon or evening, spend a night in or near Pointe-à-Pitre and cross to Marie-Galante the next morning. You will arrive rested, with the whole day ahead to discover the villa and dive into the pool.

The boat from Pointe-à-Pitre

This is the most common option. Fast shuttles leave from the Bergevin ferry terminal in Pointe-à-Pitre and reach Marie-Galante in about one hour. Several crossings run every day, both morning and afternoon. Most arrive at the port of Grand-Bourg, the island's main town, just a few minutes from the villa.

A few tips for a pleasant crossing:

  • Book ahead, especially during school holidays, long weekends and the Terre de Blues festival.
  • Check timetables directly with the ferry companies: they change with the day and the season.
  • The channel can be choppy, as the crossing is open to the Atlantic. If you are prone to seasickness, sit at the back, in the open air, and keep your eyes on the horizon.
  • Arrive early for boarding, with your luggage clearly labelled.

The Bergevin terminal is about fifteen minutes by car from the airport. A taxi will take you there easily.

Bringing your car: the car ferry

If you have rented a car in Guadeloupe and want to keep it, some companies run ferry crossings that carry vehicles. The trip is a little longer and car spaces are limited, so booking is essential. Check with your rental company too, as not all of them allow their vehicles to travel to another island.

In most cases, it is simpler to cross as a foot passenger and rent a car directly on Marie-Galante.

The plane: the fastest way

Marie-Galante has a small airfield near Grand-Bourg. Short flights connect it to Pôle Caraïbes airport: the trip takes only about twenty minutes, with a stunning view over the lagoon and the islands. It is the ideal option if you want to connect straight from your long-haul flight without going through the ferry terminal.

Bear in mind that the aircraft are small and baggage is often weight-restricted. Flight frequency may also vary by season. Check the conditions carefully with the airline before booking, especially if you are travelling with diving gear or large suitcases.

On the island: renting a car

On Marie-Galante, a car is almost essential. Public transport is scarce, and the finest beaches, distilleries and windmills are spread all over the island. Don't worry: the island is small, and you can reach any point in about half an hour.

  • Book your car before you leave, at the same time as your crossing. In high season, available cars go fast.
  • Ask for delivery at the port or airfield: most rental companies offer it, so you can drive off straight away with your luggage.
  • Think about group size: for twelve people, two cars or a minibus are often needed.
  • Drive gently: roads are narrow, and you may meet an ox cart or a herd of goats along the way.

Scooters and e-bikes are also lovely options for short outings, as long as you avoid the hottest hours of the day.

In short

A flight to Pointe-à-Pitre, one hour by boat or twenty minutes by plane, and a car waiting on arrival: getting to Marie-Galante is easier than it looks. Book early, keep your schedule a little flexible, and let the island's rhythm take over as soon as the boat docks.
